Personal Research Notes — RAG + Agents


2026-04-12

Spent the morning tuning chunk size. Started at 256 tokens, tried 512,
ended at 768. Going from 256 to 512 noticeably improved retrieval —
fewer chunks where the answer is technically present but the
surrounding context is missing. Going from 512 to 768 didn't seem to
help much and made the top-k results longer than they needed to be.
Sticking with 512 for now.


2026-04-15

Question of the week: when should the agent decline to answer?

Right now the system prompt says "if the retrieved documents don't
contain the answer, say so explicitly". This works most of the time
but the model is too eager — even when the docs DO contain the
answer, if it's not phrased exactly the way the question is, the
model says "I don't know". Lowering the threshold means more
hallucinations. The right answer is probably reranking + confidence
scoring; that's a Phase 2 problem.


2026-04-20

Notes from re-reading the original RAG paper (Lewis et al., 2020):

- They used a Wikipedia dump as the corpus and DPR (dense passage
  retrieval) as the retriever. Both choices look quaint now — modern
  systems use general-purpose embedding models that weren't trained
  specifically on retrieval data.
- The "RAG-Sequence" variant retrieves once and uses the same docs
  for every output token. The "RAG-Token" variant re-retrieves at
  each step. Production systems almost universally use RAG-Sequence
  because per-token retrieval is expensive and rarely improves
  quality.
- The paper's headline result was that RAG beats a closed-book LM on
  knowledge-intensive tasks. The interesting result for engineers is
  that the retrieval quality dominated the final answer quality —
  better embeddings beat a bigger LLM in every ablation.


2026-05-03

Things I want to test in the next eval pass:

1. Does reranking help? (Cohere rerank-v3 vs no rerank)
2. Does query expansion help? (Reformulate "What's X?" into 3 paraphrases)
3. Does the model improve when we feed it the SECTION (h2 header) of
   each retrieved chunk along with the text?

Hypothesis: #3 is the biggest win and #1 is overrated for personal-
sized corpora.


2026-05-18

Personal observation: when the corpus is small (< 100 docs), the
choice of embedding model matters far less than I expected. Even a
naive bag-of-words retriever scores well. The real value of dense
embeddings shows up at corpus size > 10k where lexical overlap fails.
